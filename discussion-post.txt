Mid-Module Check-In: Late Night Radio

Try it here: https://hazeljiang0107-del.github.io/mstu5003-responding_to_the_world/

For this check-in I built Late Night Radio, a bright pixel art radio that you tune by hand in the middle of the night, trying to find someone out there. Every control is a paper note clipped to a clothesline: tuning, antenna, volume, time of night, and a call-in box. No single input does much on its own. The interesting part is how they combine. The frequency and the antenna angle together decide the signal strength, so getting only one of them right still leaves you in static. When the signal is weak, the words on the screen break apart into random symbols and the music warbles. The signal also slowly drifts away, so you have to keep nudging the dial, and you can smack the radio, which helps most of the time but sometimes makes it worse. If you call in while the signal is bad, the host reads your broken message back very seriously and calls it deep. There is also a secret station that only exists at 3 AM on 99.9 with the antenna pointing straight up. Every station you hold clearly drops a new note onto the Station Log rope, and you can flip the unknown notes to get a hint.

I wanted the radio to feel like being awake when everyone else is asleep. It starts out lonely, then becomes comforting once you find a voice. It is delightfully frustrating because the signal never stays put, and satisfying when the static finally snaps into clear words. The biggest reward is for patience. If you hold a clean signal long enough, the whole radio glows warm, the host thanks you for staying, and the drift calms down. My idea is that being heard happens when you keep listening.

For the interface, I used sliders for tuning and the antenna because I wanted people to search instead of picking from a menu, and I used three buttons for the time of night because it is a setting rather than something you fine tune. You can also drag the antenna directly, scroll on the dial, or use the arrow keys. Hanging the controls on a rope makes them feel handmade and alive, since they sway on their own, swing when you touch them, and dance faster when a station locks in. I chose a bright and colorful style on purpose so the night feels hopeful instead of gloomy.

I built this with an AI coding assistant. The prompt that solved the core logic was asking it to make the signal strength from the frequency and the antenna so that being right on only one of them gives a weak signal. The answer was to multiply the two scores instead of adding them. Playtesting caught two problems that the code alone did not show. At first the drift was so strong that after about twenty seconds nothing could lock at all, which felt broken rather than fun. The warm state also reset at every tiny dip in the signal, so it was almost impossible to reach. Both were fixed by adjusting the numbers while actually playing it.

I am still figuring out where the line is between wanting to keep tending the radio and finding it annoying, whether the hint notes give away too much, and whether the host should remember your call later so it feels more like a relationship than a single moment.
